### New engine and loader features
Changes are in [corridor.js](resources/js/sim/corridor.js), [engine.js](resources/js/sim/engine.js), [car.js](resources/js/sim/car.js) and the renderers.

- **Road shapes:**
  - T-junctions, from a stub length per end (`stubStartM` / `stubEndM`).
  - Curved roads through several junctions.
  - Arterials at an angle (`headingDeg`).
  - Drawn-only roads.
- **`joins`** — one road carrying on into the next. It supports:
  - a plain end-to-start handover;
  - a partway merge into the kerb lane, where merging cars give way;
  - a partway peel-off.

  Cars queue across a join for the next signal. Sensors, all-way stops and signal lookahead also work across it. Lanes line up at the median, and handovers glide with no visible jump.
- **Lane behaviour:**
  - Cars in an ending lane move over early (160 m before the end).
  - A car holds its lane for 3 s after turning.
  - When two cars reach a merge together, the one waiting longest goes first.
- **Turns into a wider road** land in the matching lane directly. Where a road gains a lane just past a junction, the stop line sits further back (`stopLineBackM`).
- **Lane-drop tapers** are drawn (`tapers`).
- **Narrow road feeding a wider approach** (Grosvenor and Festival at Burnett, Duxbury at Lynnwood) — only the lanes that actually exist get arrows, a stop line and a signal head, and cars only use those lanes (`coveredLanes`).

## 3. Fixed-time and green wave now reach a steady state

On the new network, fixed-time and green-wave waits grew for the whole of a 2-hour run. Adaptive was fine. The cause was the inputs to Webster's formula; the formula itself is untouched.

- **Saturation flow set to 1250 veh/h/lane everywhere**, down from the textbook 1800–1900.
  - That is what the simulated cars actually discharge: measured on a deep straight-through queue at the single intersection with 30 s greens.
  - Applied to all five corridor JSONs.
  - It is now the loader's default, `MODEL_SATURATION_FLOW_PER_LANE_PER_HOUR` in [corridor.js](resources/js/sim/corridor.js). [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md) is updated to match.
  - Franco's saved Road Editor copies (`corridor_layouts` 1, 5, 6, 7, 8) were updated too, saturation value only.
  - **Row 2**, another account's copy of the small corridor, is not updated.
- **Webster plans on the busiest lane**, not the approach's flow spread evenly over its lanes.
  - [engine.js](resources/js/sim/engine.js): `criticalLaneShare()` and `_criticalLaneShareAt()` spread each movement over only the lanes allowed to carry it.
  - The old even split understated the load where lane markings push most cars into one lane. Burnett & Grosvenor and Lynnwood & Duxbury were about 2× out.
  - Where lane use is even, the result is unchanged.
- **Demand trims** on hatfield-realistic: southern Jan Shoba 2–6 → 2–5, and Lynnwood 2–10 → 2–8.
- **Result** over 2 hours on three seeds: fixed-time waits hold at about 16–22 s (they used to climb to about 80 s), and green wave at about 13–17 s. The small corridor also stopped creeping.

## 4. Automatic warm-up and faster batches

### Settling detector
[plateau.js](resources/js/sim/plateau.js) is rewritten. The old version compared the two halves of the rest of the run, and gave 0 s, 300 s or 3000 s for curves that visibly settled between 600 and 2400 s. The new version works in two steps:

- **Where to cut:** MSER, a standard rule — it cuts the run where the rest has the smallest standard error, searching the first half only.
- **Whether it's steady after the cut:** a trend check on what's left. It must not drift more than 15% over one measured run (40 min), or the drift must be indistinguishable from noise.

A simpler "stays within ±15%" rule was also tested and rejected: its answers were erratic.

### Warm-up probe
New file: [warmupProbe.js](resources/js/sim/warmupProbe.js). It runs before every batch, from the /results button and from the command line.

- **Probe runs:** each controller runs for 2 hours from empty, on the batch's first 3 normal-power seeds, and the three runs are averaged.
- **Warm-up chosen:** the slowest settling point × 1.5, never less than 6 min and never more than 60 min.
- **If a controller never settles:** the batch uses 60 min and is flagged not stationary.
- **Measured so far:** about 45 min for hatfield-realistic, and up to the 60-min maximum for the small corridor, whose waits creep for 2 hours (the old "6 min is enough" verdict for it was wrong).
- **Command line** ([runBatch.mjs](batch/runBatch.mjs)): `--warmup-ticks=auto` is now the default, and `--probe-only` prints the verdict and stops.
- [warmupDiagnostics.mjs](batch/warmupDiagnostics.mjs): new `--windows` flag, and output updated for the new detector.

### Stored with every run
- **New migrations:**
  - `2026_09_25_000002`: adds `warmup_ticks` and `warmup_stationary` to `simulation_runs`.
  - `2026_09_25_000003`: adds `batch_started_at`, taken before the warm-up probe starts.
- **Wired through:** [SimulationRun.php](app/Models/SimulationRun.php), [StoreSimulationRunRequest.php](app/Http/Requests/StoreSimulationRunRequest.php), [SimulationRunController.php](app/Http/Controllers/SimulationRunController.php) (stores the start time in UTC) and [apiPayload.js](resources/js/sim/apiPayload.js).
- Both migrations have been run locally.

### Parallel batches in the browser
[results.js](resources/js/results.js) and [batchWorker.js](resources/js/sim/batchWorker.js):

- **Worker pool:** a `BatchWorkerPool` runs one worker per CPU core, less one for the page, up to 12 workers. The warm-up probe runs and the 360 batch runs are spread across it.
- **Same results as one at a time:** each run keeps its own seed, results are taken in matrix order, and each condition's recovery curve is still built rep by rep.
- **Memory:** only about three pool-widths of runs are started ahead of the one being saved.
- **Speed:** the first full batch on the small corridor took about 8 minutes for the 360 runs. The earlier estimate, one at a time, was 57 min.
- **Command line:** the command-line batch still runs one at a time.
